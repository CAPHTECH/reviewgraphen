# ADR 0035: Cross-family issue-worthiness study on FSL HEAD

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-08-15
- Supersedes: ADR 0033/0034 only as the endpoint for a new additive experiment

## Context

The `m7-head-v1` test/fix generator passed 1 of 20 known-defect calibration
units. Its post-hoc test-only rescore removed unified-diff application as a
construct-irrelevant bottleneck, reconstructed all twenty tests without another
model draw, and passed 2 of 20. Test generation was an experimental measurement
instrument supplied by the benchmark, not a ReviewGraphen capability. It did
not function well enough even when a canonical fix and known regression test
existed.

A new endpoint counts findings that an AI judge labels
`issue_should_be_created`. This is closer to the operator decision the study
wants to support, but it is weaker than mechanical verification. Model
agreement is not evidence that a correctness defect exists, and same-family
generation and judgment can share correlated errors.

## Decision

Create `m7-head-issue-v1` without changing any prior result.

### Authority and external effects

The endpoint is an explicitly non-authority model disposition with three
values: `issue_should_be_created`, `should_not_be_created`, and
`unable_to_decide`. It never becomes accepted evidence, verification, a
ReviewGraphen gate decision, or a statement that a defect is confirmed. No
external issue or other upstream object is created.

Raw responses and parsed dispositions are retained with backend, model,
settings, prompt/tool-policy version, exact input inventory, and hashes.

### Matched judge calibration

Before production, both judge families receive the same forty opaque cases
derived from the twenty `m7-real-v1` presence-verified units.

For each unit, a deterministic candidate states that the production behavior at
the target root does not satisfy an attached regression scenario. The same
scenario and claim are paired with:

- the known defective parent production source as the positive case; and
- the canonical fix production source as the target-specific negative case.

The presence evidence establishes that the exact scenario fails on the parent
and passes on the fix. The negative label concerns only persistence of that
known target defect; it does not assert that the fix revision contains no other
defects. Free-form control findings are not negative labels because ADR 0026
correctly leaves them unlabeled.

The judge sees neither label, parent/fix role, revision, pair relationship,
commit or issue metadata, nor benchmark arm. Case identifiers are independently
content-addressed and ordering is hash-sorted. Test file names and issue
references are redacted. Production files are complete selected files with the
same recorded path set on both sides.

This calibration is deliberately optimistic relative to novel production
findings: a known regression scenario is stronger evidence than ordinary model
prose. It measures evidence-conditioned known-case classification, not general
precision on previously unknown findings.

Each of the Codex and Claude judges must independently meet all four thresholds:

- positive sensitivity at least 0.75;
- target-specific negative specificity at least 0.75;
- balanced accuracy at least 0.75; and
- unable-to-decide rate at most 0.20.

Failure by either judge stops the experiment. Thresholds and packets are frozen
before any calibration call.

### Symmetric cross-family production assignment

If calibration passes, freeze the FSL HEAD revision and inventory every
production Rust file. Primary ownership assigns each production file to exactly
one deterministic size-bounded packet; recorded cross-file context may be
duplicated. There is no source-file sampling.

Every B1, G3-proxy, and full ReviewGraphen packet is reviewed once by each
generator family under the same model settings used across arms. Findings
generated by Codex are judged only by Claude; findings generated by Claude are
judged only by Codex. Thus every arm has the same two opposite-family paths.
The judge sees neither arm nor generator family.

B1, G3-proxy, and full ReviewGraphen differ only in their frozen scaffold.
ReviewGraphen findings remain non-authority proposals. Issue-worthiness
judgment does not promote them.

### Reporting

Report for each arm and generator/judge stratum:

- total findings and total operator reading units;
- issue-should-be-created count and fraction;
- should-not and unable counts;
- content-linked cross-arm overlap and arm-unique findings; and
- the complete known-case calibration confusion matrix.

The report compares its model-disposition endpoint to the earlier
`valid_novel_defect` model dispositions but does not relabel those historical
results or claim a verified-defect comparison. Zero yield is reported as zero.
Recall is not estimated.

## Consequences

- The experiment can compare review scaffolds without relying on the failed
  regression-test generator.
- Its endpoint is less authoritative than mechanical verification and must
  always be displayed with judge calibration.
- Cross-family assignment reduces direct self-consistency but does not prove
  independence; both families may share training data, conventions, or errors.
- A calibration pass on scenario-backed known cases may overstate performance
  on prose-only current-HEAD findings.
- The full source scope measures this frozen FSL revision only.
