## 3. Can the series resume from trial 2, or must it restart?

**It can resume from `noskill-1`, provided both identity gates still pass.**

- Trials are independent by construction: fresh scratch, fresh
  `CARGO_TARGET_DIR`, fresh credential-free `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`, fresh
  sandbox. Nothing carries between them.
- The alternating order and 3-per-arm balance are preserved by continuing at
  `noskill-1`; nothing in the preregistration requires one contiguous
  session.
- `skill-1` remains valid: it ran entirely inside the frozen window, with
  both gates passed and the cache credited.

**It must restart from trial 1 if either pinned hash has changed** —
listing `afc7adb7…` or health `6cdd746c…`. A changed hash is a changed
condition, `skill-1` would not be poolable with what follows, and that needs
a fresh preregistration rather than a resumption. The gate enforces this on
its own: it refuses before any request, as it did on 2026-08-20 when
`mode` went `dspark` → `baseline`.

Resuming also needs a new frozen window from the operator, since the
previous one has closed.

## 4. The counterfactual discrepancy — and a correction to the figure

**The ~1,468 s figure is not the applicable one.** It was v3's, and it was
declared **void** in `preregistration-v4.json` before this run, because it
assumed `dspark` *and* a full-recompute prefill that `RECONCILIATION.md`
showed was never paid.

The preregistered counterfactual for this run is **~3,017 s**. Observed
**4,078 s**. Over by **1.35×**, not 2.8×.

### 4.1 Time budget, derived from the retained stream, no new requests

53 API calls, span 3,955 s of the 4,078 s recorded elapsed.

| | calls | output tok | time | rate | share |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| deep (>1,000 out tok) | 14 | 53,213 | 2,988 s | **17.8 tok/s** | 76% |
| shallow | 39 | 7,534 | 967 s | 7.8 tok/s | 24% |

- **Prefill was not the cost.** 47 of 52 calls completed in less time than a
  full recompute of their own context would take. Summed contexts are
  4,152,395 tokens, which at 311 tok/s would be 13,352 s — more than three
  times the entire run. The cache credited, as the in-flight check said
  (ratio 0.305).
- **Thinking share 94.6%** — 229,904 chars of thinking against 13,083 of
  final text, at a context depth reaching ~102k. Consistent with the
  operator's 62% at 30k and the depth trend.
- **Deep decode 17.8 tok/s against the operator's baseline figure of 16.5.**
  Close agreement, and 4.0× the 4.5 tok/s the same arm managed under
  `dspark`. Baseline delivered exactly what it promised.

### 4.2 Why it still overran, and the miss is not in the wrong direction

Baseline **was** faster. The overrun is entirely output volume:

| | dspark (v2 `skill-1`) | baseline (v4 `skill-1`) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| total output tokens | 20,264 | **71,611** (3.5×) |
| deep output tokens | 14,435 | **53,213** (3.7×) |
| deep decode rate | 4.5 tok/s | **17.8 tok/s** (4.0×) |
| outcome | timed out at the cap | **completed, verified** |

The counterfactual held output volume fixed at the dspark run's 14,435 deep
tokens and scaled only the rate. The actual run reasoned 3.7× more. At the
observed 17.8 tok/s, 53,213 deep tokens is 2,989 s — matching the measured
2,988 s to within a second.

So the model is not slower than predicted; it did far more work. The
preregistration named this in advance as the failure mode of the
projection: *"unbounded in another: if a trial reasons more than skill-1
did, the deep term grows without limit — that is the term that hit the cap."*
It grew, and the run still finished inside the cap.

**Recorded as an open item for resume:** whether that 3.5× output increase
is a property of the baseline decode path, of this task at this depth, or of
one trial's variance cannot be told from n=1. It is the single most
important thing the remaining five trials would settle.
